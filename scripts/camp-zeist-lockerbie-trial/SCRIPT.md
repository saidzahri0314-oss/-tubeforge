> In November 1991, Scotland and the United States charged two men: Abdelbaset al-Megrahi and Lamin Khalifah Fhimah. Both worked for Libyan Arab Airlines in Malta — and both, prosecutors said, were Libyan intelligence officers.

> Libya refused to hand them over. The UN Security Council imposed sanctions: an air embargo, an arms embargo, frozen assets.

> Because the case against Megrahi leaned heavily on one man's memory, and one date.

## ACT VII · A VERDICT, NOT AN ENDING — `8:22 – 9:58`

> **VO**
>
> In 2007, the Scottish Criminal Cases Review Commission — which investigates possible wrongful convictions — said Megrahi may have suffered a miscarriage of justice. It found documents that had never been disclosed to his defence, suggesting Gauci knew of a large American reward before he first picked out Megrahi's photograph. After the trial, reward money was paid to Gauci and his brother.
>
> The case went back to the appeal court, but never reached a verdict. Megrahi had terminal prostate cancer. In August 2009 he dropped it, and Scotland released him on compassionate grounds, with roughly three months to live. He was welcomed home to Tripoli with flags. >
> Libya had formally accepted responsibility for "the actions of its officials" in 2003, and paid compensation to the families. Within months, its prime minister was telling the BBC that the money had been paid to buy peace — not as an admission of guilt.
>
> The families split. Many American relatives believe justice was done. Others — most prominently Dr Jim Swire, whose daughter Flora was on the plane — came to believe Megrahi was innocent. So did Robert Black, the professor who designed the court.
>
> In 2021, Scotland's appeal court looked at the case one more time. It upheld the conviction.

## AUDIO DESIGN

- **The drone never resolves.** One low drone in A — Scottish smallpipes, not Highland pipes, and never a melody — runs under the whole film. The harmony above it keeps reaching for a resolution and never gets one. The last chord is suspended and cut off.
- **Gold has a sound.** A newly composed celesta figure (carol-like, but *not* a real carol; recordings of real carols are a rights problem) plays only when gold is on screen: the window in `00A`, Lockerbie's lights in `02D`, the fragment in `03G`, the court windows in `05A`, Sliema in Act VI, and the final shot.
- **Act I drops the score** from the radar shot `02C` to the end of `02G`. Wind and the radar sweep only. The 3 s beat under `02F` is **true digital silence**, not room tone. It's the most important three seconds in the film.
- **The courtroom is dry.** Small-room reverb, paper, chair creaks, the hum of the interpreters' booth. **No gavels** — Scottish courts don't use them. The judges' entrance in `06A` gets the macer's call, not a bang.
- **Act VI is the only act that swings.** The celesta toggles on and off with the lights in `07C`, in sync with each frame flip.
- **Act VIII** is modern room tone: HVAC hum, a far-off door, no drone. The drone returns on the first frame of the Close.
- The celesta returns alone under `10C`–`10D` and **stops mid-phrase on the cut to black**.
