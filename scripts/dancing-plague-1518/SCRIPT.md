## ACT V · WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED — `5:28 – 6:48`

> **VO**
>
> The shoes were rubbed with holy oil. The dancers were led around the wooden carving. Mass was said over them. And by early September, the dancing stopped.
>
> So what actually happened here?
>
> Theory one — the one you'll see repeated everywhere: ergot. A fungus that grows on damp rye and produces alkaloids chemically related to LSD. Bad harvest, wet grain, poisoned bread, hallucinating city.
>
> It's a great story. It doesn't work. Ergot poisoning *constricts* your blood vessels. It causes convulsions and gangrene — sometimes badly enough that fingers and toes drop off. It does not let you dance, coherently, in time, in a crowd, for days. And it doesn't explain why hundreds of people all did the same specific thing.
>
> Theory two: a secret heretical cult, dancing as ritual. There's no evidence for it, and it doesn't fit people begging their neighbours to make it stop.
>
> Which leaves the explanation most historians now accept. And it's stranger than either of them.
>
> These people were in a trance.

## ACT VI · WHY IT WORKED — `6:48 – 7:44`

> **VO**

> To understand it, you have to know what 1518 felt like in Alsace.
>

The countryside had been simmering with peasant conspiracies for a generation. Chroniclers describe families abandoning their children, and the desperate drowning themselves in the Rhine.
>
> And in that region, everybody knew one specific thing about Saint Vitus: that he could punish you by seizing your body and making you dance.
>
> *(BEAT — 2 s)*

> That's the missing piece. Take a population under extreme, sustained stress. Give them an absolute, shared belief that a supernatural force can take control of their limbs. And then show them one person it has already happened to.
>
> The belief supplies the script. The stress supplies the trigger. The body does the rest.

## AUDIO DESIGN

- **The drum is the villain.** One tabor drum runs the entire film. It starts at **60 bpm** under the cold open and creeps to **132 bpm** by Act III. The audience should not consciously notice the acceleration.
- **Act IV drops the drum entirely** at the ban — hard silence for 2 s before the wagon sequence. That silence is the biggest moment in the film.
- **Act V (theory section)** swaps to clean, dry, no reverb — signals "we are out of the story and into the analysis."
- The drum returns for the final shot, alone, and stops mid-bar.
- No modern instruments anywhere. Tabor, shawm, hurdy-gurdy, voice.
